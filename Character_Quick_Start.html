Character Quick Start Sheet

Chicago 2089 · Make Your Character

Ten steps, one sitting. Open the Character Sheet next to this page: every step tells you what to choose, then exactly where it goes on the sheet. Every rule here comes straight from the Mechanics Compendium, which has the full detail.

Step 1Concept

The ruleOne sentence. "A sex worker in the Shade who runs a Face's game, with a voice mod and an arm sword she calls Dr. Lobotomy." "A clan botanist trying to solve the medicine problem." That sentence drives every choice below.

Everything else follows from this sentence. Decide who this person is before you look at any numbers; every later step has an option that fits a good concept.

On the sheetUnder Who, fill in Name, Pronouns, and Concept, in one sentence. Leave Level at Level 1.

Step 2Tradition & Path

The ruleChoose one of eight Traditions. It gives you starting Evasion and Hit Points, a kit, a class feature, a Hope Feature, and two domains. Then choose one of its two Paths; you get the Path's Foundation feature now and unlock its Specialization and Mastery later.

Your Tradition is your class. Your Path is your specialty within it: a Solo is a Hitter or a Bodyguard, a Hacker is a Runner or a Deep Seer, a Nomad is a Coyote or a Fabricator. Each Path has three features. You start with the Foundation. At tier 2 you can spend an advancement to take the Specialization, and at tier 3 another to take the Mastery.

Your Tradition is your class: it sets your Evasion and Hit Points, your two card domains, your kit, and two features that are always yours. Your Path is your subclass. Open a Tradition below to read it.

NomadEvasion 10 · Hit Points 6 · Ghost and Forge

The road is infrastructure, and you keep it running. You've survived by knowing things the city forgot how to know.

Class Feature · Clan Road
The clans will help you: a safe route, a place to sleep, a message that leaves no trace, a name in a city you've never seen. Keep a Clan Standing score on your sheet from −3 to +3. It goes up when you do the clans a favor and down when you take more than you give; at −3 they stop answering until you make it right. Once per long rest, an urgent request goes through in hours instead of days.
Hope Feature · Road Law Spend 3 Hope
Spend 3 Hope: one roll to drive, find your way, build, or survive succeeds with Hope, and every ally within Close range clears a Stress.

Paths: choose one

Coyote

Play the Coyote if you want to get people through.

You start with Under the Wire. Sensors get no return from you: aug scanners, drone optics, and biometric gates read nothing. Mark a Stress to move anywhere within Far range along a route no sensor covers, bringing one ally with you. You're both Hidden from machines when you arrive.

Later: Dead Zone (specialization · tier 2), Gone (mastery · tier 3).

Fabricator

Play the Fabricator if you want the crew's gear to exist in no database anywhere.

You start with Fab Access. Anything you build reads as unknown to every corporate scanner and can't be tracked, jammed, or hacked. A clan garage answers you within a day anywhere in the metropolis: somewhere to build, somewhere to hide a vehicle, somewhere nobody's camera reaches.

Later: Field Line (specialization · tier 2), The Doctrine (mastery · tier 3).

SoloEvasion 11 · Hit Points 7 · Iron and Command

You are the most dangerous person in this room. You've learned to carry that fact quietly, because the ones who announce it are amateurs, and amateurs are expensive.

Class Feature · Threat Clock
You see a fight coming before the first move. You're never surprised when things turn violent. Spend a Hope when it kicks off: you act first, before any adversary, and nobody in your crew is caught flat-footed. The GM can't spend Fear to go before you.
Hope Feature · The Professional Spend 3 Hope
Spend 3 Hope: clear 2 Armor Slots, and your next attack this scene has advantage.

Paths: choose one

Hitter

Play the Hitter if you want to be the one who ends fights.

You start with Momentum. On a roll with Hope, you can bank a d6 on this card instead of gaining a Hope, up to your Proficiency. When you make an attack roll or a damage roll, spend any number of them and add what they show. At the end of each session, clear any left and gain a Hope per die cleared.

Later: Closer (specialization · tier 2), Finish It (mastery · tier 3).

Bodyguard

Play the Bodyguard if you want to be the reason nobody else gets hurt.

You start with Principal. At the start of a scene, name your principal: an ally or a client. While you're within Very Close range of them you have +1 Armor Score, and when they're attacked you can mark a Stress to take the attack instead.

Later: Answer (specialization · tier 2), Not Today (mastery · tier 3).

HackerEvasion 11 · Hit Points 5 · Wire and Archive

Every network has a shape. Most people see the surface — the login screens, the error messages, the access denied. You see the plumbing.

Class Feature · Persistent Access
Once you've broken into a network, you can always get back in. Whenever a network's Progress countdown completes with you on it, you take a Backdoor there in addition to your prize. Write it on your sheet. Once per long rest, walk straight back through one of your Backdoors and start where you left off, with the Consequence countdown where it was when you left.
Hope Feature · Clean Run Spend 3 Hope
Spend 3 Hope: one deck roll succeeds with Hope, and the Consequence countdown doesn't tick this action.

Paths: choose one

Runner

Play the Runner if you want to hack at gunfight pace.

You start with Quick Draw. +1 to deck attacks in a fight. Once per scene, fire off a quickhack at someone within Close range as part of attacking or moving, instead of as its own action.

Later: Hot Deck (specialization · tier 2), Double Tap (mastery · tier 3).

Deep Seer

Play the Deep Seer if you want to go where the net stops being a place.

You start with Deep Hands. In any network or zone, when you succeed with Hope on a deck roll, tick Progress one extra. Your frag Experiences are +3 instead of +2. Once per long rest, thread the whole crew through a contested zone of your tier or lower without a fight.

Later: Standing Feed (specialization · tier 2), Switchboard (mastery · tier 3).

FaceEvasion 10 · Hit Points 5 · Voice and Ledger

People are a skill. Reading them, managing how they see you, knowing who to call and how to ask: all of it can be learned, and you learned it.

Class Feature · Room
The moment you walk in, you know who actually runs the room, who's nervous, and who's about to leave. Spend a Hope: the GM tells you what the room wants and who you need to get past to get it.
Hope Feature · The Read Spend 3 Hope
Spend 3 Hope: one Presence Roll against a person succeeds with Hope, and the GM tells you what that person is most afraid of right now.

Paths: choose one

Broker

Play the Broker if you want everyone to owe you.

You start with Middleman. When two parties make a deal through you, you gain a Hope and a fistful of dollars. Once per long rest, name what the crew needs and a price for it, and someone who can pay that price does — then the GM names one thing about them: they want something back, they're hard to reach, they're already in trouble, or you didn't part on good terms.

Later: Markers (specialization · tier 2), Everyone Owes You (mastery · tier 3).

Chameleon

Play the Chameleon if you want to be whoever the door needs.

You start with Second Face. You have one fake identity with real paper and a real history. It passes any automated check without a roll, and you can mark a Stress to have it pass a human one.

Later: Quick Change (specialization · tier 2), Nobody (mastery · tier 3).

CorpEvasion 10 · Hit Points 5 · Ledger and Command

You know how a company really works, because you were inside one, or you studied them so closely it makes no difference.

Class Feature · Paper
You can tell at a glance what a contract, a permit, or a badge really lets someone do, and what it doesn't. Spend a Hope: the GM tells you who signed it and what it would take to get them to sign yours.
Hope Feature · Paper Trail Spend 3 Hope
Spend 3 Hope: one roll that leans on a document, a badge, or an authorization succeeds with Hope, and the document you needed exists for the scene.

Paths: choose one

Exec

Play the Exec if you want the company to do what you say.

You start with Signing Authority. In any corporate space, you have advantage on Presence Rolls to give orders to anyone who looks junior to you. Once per scene, a staffer does one small thing you tell them to, no roll.

Later: Budget (specialization · tier 2), C-Suite (mastery · tier 3).

Bagman

Play the Bagman if you believe everyone has a price and you know how to pay it.

You start with Grease. You know who can be bought before you ask; the GM tells you when someone can't. When you bribe someone, the Presence Roll has +2. If it fails, you can mark a Stress to make it a failure with Hope: they say no, but nobody's offended and the GM gains no Fear.

Later: Clean Money (specialization · tier 2), Everyone Has a Price (mastery · tier 3).

MediaEvasion 10 · Hit Points 5 · Archive and Voice

The story is what happens after the fact is over. Getting the fact is the easy part.

Class Feature · Platform
You have an audience. Anything you publish gets seen by people who matter, and people who matter know you can publish. Spend a Hope: someone who doesn't want to be on camera does what you ask to stay off it.
Hope Feature · Live Spend 3 Hope
Spend 3 Hope: one roll made on camera succeeds with Hope, and every adversary in the scene who cares about being seen marks a Stress.

Paths: choose one

Investigator

Play the Investigator if you want to know first.

You start with Leads. Keep a Leads count on your sheet, up to your Instinct plus 2. Gain one whenever you find a clue. Spend one to ask the GM a yes-or-no question about a person, a place, or an outfit you already have a clue about, and get a true answer.

Later: Corroborate (specialization · tier 2), Follow the Money (mastery · tier 3).

Icon

Play the Icon if you want a city full of people who will show up for you.

You start with Following. People show up for you. Each of these once per long rest, and you say how the word goes out.
• Turn Out: a crowd fills a public space within Far range for the scene — cover, confusion, and nobody moves through it fast.
• Drown It Out: for the rest of the scene, nothing said in the open here is clearly heard, recorded, or reported straight.
• Point Them: name someone in public. Every face turns, and they mark 2 Stress.

Later: Crowd Work (specialization · tier 2), Movement (mastery · tier 3).

TechieEvasion 11 · Hit Points 5 · Forge and Wire

The machine doesn't lie. It does exactly what its instruction set tells it to do. The art is in reading the instruction set, not the machine.

Class Feature · Direct Command
Mark a Stress: for the scene, one machine you have access to (yours, or one you've rewritten or hijacked) does exactly what you tell it instead of what it was programmed to do. It's still the same machine; it just has your judgment now. For a machine you don't have access to, see Root Access.
Hope Feature · Root Access Spend 3 Hope
Spend 3 Hope: one roll to command, fix, rewrite, or hack a machine succeeds with Hope, and any machine within Close range obeys one order right now, whether you have access or not.

Paths: choose one

Rigger

Play the Rigger if you want a machine that's yours.

You start with Companion. The Scout Drone from your kit is more than a drone: it's your companion, or swap it for a Micro Drone, a Crawler, a Patrol Drone, or a vehicle fitted with a Jack Control Port, and take that chassis from the table below. It gets its own sheet: Difficulty 10 plus its chassis bonus, a d6 attack, two Experiences at +2, 3 Stress, and one training option per level. Commanding it is a Knowledge Roll; spend a Hope to add one of its Experiences. When it would take damage it marks Stress instead, and when its Stress is full it limps off until your next long rest.

Later: Uplink (specialization · tier 2), War Machine (mastery · tier 3).

Ripper

Play the Ripper if you want to build people.

You start with Wired. One extra augment slot, already filled with a second Tier 1 augment (or a Tier 3 one, if your story explains it). Once per long rest, swap what's in that slot for any augment you have on hand, at any bench.

Later: Off the Books (specialization · tier 2), Overbuilt (mastery · tier 3).

CleanerEvasion 12 · Hit Points 5 · Iron and Ghost

The city has blind spots and you live in them. A Solo is hired to be seen coming; you're hired so nobody knows anyone came at all.

Class Feature · From Nowhere
When you hit a target while you're Hidden, or while an ally is within Melee range of it, add a d6 per tier to the damage.
Hope Feature · Vanish Spend 3 Hope
Spend 3 Hope: one roll to go unseen, get somewhere unseen, or strike from Hidden succeeds with Hope, and when it resolves you're Hidden again.

Paths: choose one

Stalker

Play the Stalker if you want the first hit to be the last.

You start with First Strike. The first time each scene you hit someone, double the damage. Name a target at the start of a job: rolls to find, follow, or reach them have advantage.

Later: Death Strike (specialization · tier 2), True Strike (mastery · tier 3).

Troubleshooter

Play the Troubleshooter if you want to be the one they call when it has already gone wrong.

You start with Groundwork. You never walk in cold. At the start of a job take Groundwork tokens equal to your tier plus 1. Spend one to say what you set up before you got here: a way out, a vehicle nobody can trace, a uniform that fits, a tool already in the room, or somewhere to put a body. Clear what's left at your next long rest.

Later: Contingency (specialization · tier 2), Everyone's Prepared (mastery · tier 3).

On the sheetUnder Who, choose your Tradition, then your Path. The Features section now shows your Class Feature, your Hope Feature, and all three Path features. Only the Foundation is yours at level 1; leave the I have taken this boxes on the other two unticked.

The ruleTwo picks: where you grew up (one feature) and what you were (two features).

Pick one of each. Together they give you three features.

Step 4Augmented or Pure Human

The ruleChrome or none. Both are real choices, and each gives up something the other keeps.

Interface
Augmented: Basic Neural Interface and basic optics (Kiroshi Mk.1) installed free, filling no slots: HUD, low-light vision, silent comms, deck handling, and the broadcast block that keeps NBN out.
Pure Human: A clan-fab external broadcast block. No interface.
Augment slots
Augmented: 2 at level 1; 3 at level 2; 4 at level 5; 5 at level 8.
Pure Human: None. Instead, the same number of kit slots for clan-fab gear that would otherwise need a corp interface (Section D).
Soul-rip risk
Augmented: Yes.
Pure Human: No. Extraction needs an interface.
Diving
Augmented: Yes. Any augmented character can be flashed and go under (Section C).
Pure Human: No. You hold the fridge.
Neural quickhacks
Augmented: You can be targeted.
Pure Human: Immune. There's nothing to receive.
Aug scanners
Augmented: You read.
Pure Human: You don't.
Smart weapons
Augmented: Yes.
Pure Human: No. The Power and Tech lines only (Section B).
Smart weapons aimed at you
Augmented: They lock onto your chrome.
Pure Human: Nothing to lock onto. They fire at you at disadvantage.
Decks and loads
Augmented: Yes.
Pure Human: No. A deck worn with a Crown (Section B) works at disadvantage.
Fabrication
Augmented: Normal.
Pure Human: +1 to Finesse or Knowledge Rolls to build, repair, or jury-rig.
Medicine
Augmented: Corporate clinics and pharma.
Pure Human: Clan medicine only, unless you choose otherwise. Some things it cannot treat.
On the sheetUnder Who, tap Augmented or Pure Human. For a pure human the Chrome section becomes Kit, and its slots hold clan kit items instead of augments.

Step 5Traits

The ruleDistribute +2, +1, +1, 0, 0, −1 across Agility, Strength, Finesse, Instinct, Presence, Knowledge. Your Tradition suggests a placement; ignore it if the concept says otherwise.

Agility
Sprint, leap, dodge. Running the SkyPark canopy, slipping through a crowd, shooting on the move.
Strength
Lift, smash, grapple. Breaking a door down, holding one shut, heavy weapons, gorilla arms.
Finesse
Control, hide, tinker. Pistols and blades, picking locks, building and fixing things by hand.
Instinct
Notice, sense, navigate. Reading a room for danger, spotting the camera, waiting for the shot, driving.
Presence
Charm, perform, deceive. Talking your way in, working a crowd, giving orders people follow.
Knowledge
Recall, analyze, figure out. Your deck and every quickhack, working out what's wrong, reading the paperwork, the deep net.

Step 6Numbers

The ruleCopy your Tradition's Evasion and Hit Points. Everyone starts with 6 Stress, 2 Hope, and Proficiency 1. Your damage thresholds are your armor's plus your level.

You don't calculate anything here. The sheet does it from your Tradition, your Origin, and your armor.

On the sheetCheck Right Now. Evasion and Hit Points match your Tradition (plus any Origin slot from Step 3). Stress shows 6 boxes, Hope starts with 2 held, and Proficiency is 1. Major and Severe fill in once you pick armor in Step 7.

Step 7Gear

The ruleTake the starting loadout: a primary weapon, an optional secondary, one armor, one Tier 1 augment or clan kit item, your Tradition kit, and a stack of dollars.

Everyone starts with

  • One primary weapon and, if it's one-handed, one secondary weapon, from the Tier 1 rows in Section B.
  • One armor from the Tier 1 rows in Section B.
  • One Tier 1 augment (Augmented) or one clan kit item (Pure Human), plus the free interface or block. If your character is clan or clan-adjacent, a Tier 1 power frame (Section D) can be this pick instead.
  • Your Tradition kit (Section A).
  • A stack of dollars (about $1,000: two medpatches, a burner phone, and walking-around money), and one personal item that matters.

Gear comes from two places: the starting loadout above, which you choose, and your Tradition kit below, which you get all of.

Step 9Experiences

The ruleWrite two, each at +2. A phrase, not a stat: Working Girl of the Shade, Clan Botanist, Militech Washout, Nobody Sees Me Leave.

An Experience is a word or phrase that says something specific about your operator. Each starts at +2. Before any action or reaction roll, you can spend a Hope to add a relevant Experience to the result, and more than one if more than one fits.
